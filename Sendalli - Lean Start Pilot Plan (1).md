# **Sendalli: Lean Start Pilot Plan**

*A 4-week test on one corridor in Warri/Effurun. Goal: prove that on-route keke riders will reliably carry small parcels for a fare-anchored price, and that merchants will keep sending them.*

## **1\. Core Idea**

Add a small parcel to a keke that is already going that way. The rider earns extra money for almost no extra distance (Warri fares are about N150 to N1,000), and the merchant pays far less than a N1,500 to N2,000 courier. Riders have already verbally agreed, and the plan is that they can tell passengers, "Please give me 2 minutes to hand this package to the woman waiting on the road."

## **2\. What We Are Deliberately Leaving Out**

> * Hubs (added later)  
> * Price negotiation and counters (fixed prices only)  
> * Trust badges, tiers, BVN and three-reference requirement  
> * Merchant plans and ad spots  
> * Automated WhatsApp messages to receivers  
> * Video uploads, saved routes and batch posting

Reason: the pilot answers one question first. Will on-route riders stop, load and unload for about N300, and will merchants come back? No feature list can save the idea if the answer is no.

## **3\. Setup Before Launch**

> * **One busy corridor** (for example Refinery Road to Jakpa) with 8 to 10 named drop points on the route (for example Refinery Road junction, PTI gate, Jakpa junction).  
> * **10 riders**, including the riders who already agreed, approved by their park chairman.  
> * **10 merchants** signed up personally.  
> * **Rules:** small parcels only (must fit under the seat or on a lap), sealed bags, value cap N20,000, prohibited items list, no food or perishables.  
> * **Loss policy:** Sendalli refunds up to the cap. Set aside about N10 to N20 per parcel as a loss reserve.

## **4\. Fixed Prices (Fare-Anchored)**

| Band | Typical keke fare | Parcel price | Rider | Sendalli |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Short | N150 to N300 | N200 to N250 | N150 to N200 | N50 |
| Medium | N300 to N600 | N300 to N400 | N230 to N300 | N70 to N100 |
| Long | N600 to N1,000 | N450 to N600 | N350 to N500 | N100 |

The fee is deliberately small in the shortest band. If riders do not accept quickly, raise the rider's share before changing anything else.

## **5\. The Complete Flow**

### **Merchant**

> 1. Sign up with phone plus OTP and business name (under 60 seconds).  
> 2. Tap Send parcel and pick the drop point on the corridor.  
> 3. Take a parcel photo and enter the receiver's name and phone number.  
> 4. The fixed price shows. Pay into escrow.  
> 5. The app shows a **pickup QR** and a **6-digit delivery code**. The merchant sends the code to the receiver by their own call or WhatsApp, together with the drop point and roughly when the keke will arrive.  
> 6. Hand the parcel to the rider at the roadside and let them scan the pickup QR.

### **Rider**

> 1. Sign up with phone plus OTP, name, selfie and plate photo. The park chairman approves on WhatsApp with one tap.  
> 2. Tap **On route** and choose the destination and the stops you will pass. Only on-route riders get alerts.  
> 3. Get an alert showing pickup spot, drop point, parcel photo and earnings. Tap **Accept** (first come, first served). Tap **Later** if the keke is full or a passenger is impatient, and the parcel goes to the next keke.  
> 4. Call the merchant, stop at the roadside for under a minute, check the parcel matches the photo, and scan the pickup QR.  
> 5. Call the receiver: "Jakpa junction in about 12 minutes."  
> 6. At the drop point (a 1 to 2 minute stop), check the receiver's name, ask for the 6-digit code, type it in and take a delivery photo.  
> 7. Earnings land in the in-app wallet immediately. Withdrawal to bank the same day.

### **Receiver (no app, no message from Sendalli)**

Gets the code and drop point from the merchant, waits visibly at the spot, reads out the code to the rider, and collects. If there is a problem, they tell the merchant, who reports it in the app within 30 minutes.  
**Limit:** one parcel at a time per rider at first, and one quick stop per trip so passengers are not annoyed.

## **6\. Exceptions (Decide These Now)**

> * **No rider in 15 minutes:** the post expires, the merchant is refunded and prompted to retry.  
> * **Receiver late or absent:** the keke waits 1 minute at most. The rider then continues and returns the parcel to the merchant, and keeps 50% of the fee.  
> * **Parcel does not match the photo:** the rider cancels at pickup with no penalty.  
> * **Wrong or missing code:** no release. The merchant re-shares the code.  
> * **QR fails:** the merchant reads out a backup code.  
> * **Dispute or damage:** funds are held, and Sendalli decides manually from the photos and scan timestamps.  
> * **Theft or tampering:** refund up to the cap and immediate suspension of the rider, with ID, plate and chairman on file.

## **7\. Trust and Safety at Pilot Level**

> * Riders are vouched for by the park chairman, so a bad rider costs the chairman's reputation too.  
> * Sealed bags, pickup photo and delivery photo make tampering visible.  
> * Escrow releases only on the receiver's code, so a rider who does not deliver gets nothing.  
> * Value cap limits worst-case loss. Numbers are visible to riders and merchants, so escrow and convenience are the defence against going off-app.  
> * Trust systems reduce theft, they do not eliminate it. What matters is a fast refund, a fast suspension and a paper trail.

## **8\. Rider Incentives (Never Force Acceptance)**

> * Bonus for the first 10 parcels (small, for example N500 total).  
> * Weekly bonus for 5 or more parcels.  
> * Same-day payout, which builds trust quickly. A slow first payout will spread through the park just as fast as a good one.  
> * No penalties for declining. Riders are independent, and forcing them backfires.

## **9\. What to Do Manually**

Rider approval, dispute handling, payout checks and WhatsApp support. Only automate what happens more than 20 times a day. The first 20 to 30 onboardings should be done personally to learn where people get stuck.

## **10\. Simple Income Picture**

At an average of about N70 to N100 per parcel, 25 parcels a day earns roughly N50,000 to N75,000 a month. That covers hosting, a phone and small incentives, not a salary. Treat the pilot as proof of behavior, not income. Expect thin margins once payment fees, loss reserve and incentives are counted.

## **11\. Metrics to Track for 4 Weeks**

| Metric | Target |
| :---- | :---- |
| Parcels completed per day | 10 to 25 |
| Time from post to acceptance | Under 5 minutes |
| Accept rate within 5 minutes | Over 70% |
| Parcels per rider per day | 2 or more |
| Repeat merchants sending weekly | 5 of 10 |
| Problems, disputes or off-app deals | Under 3% |
| Payout speed | Same day |

## **12\. Key Risks to Watch**

> * **Matching:** a merchant posts and no keke is passing, so they go back to their usual dispatch rider. Keep supply dense on one busy corridor and alert only riders whose route passes both the merchant and the drop point.  
> * **"They agreed" is not "they do it daily":** repeat behavior with real money is the proof.  
> * **Habit:** riders must remember to open the app and go On route. Keep it to seconds.  
> * **Stops that do not match passenger stops:** use fixed drop points so parcel stops are stops the rider would probably make anyway.  
> * **Leakage and liability:** one lost N20,000 parcel wipes out many deliveries' fees. Keep the cap low.

## **13\. Timeline**

> * **Week 0:** pick corridor and drop points, finalize prices and rules, build the simplest app, sign up the riders who already agreed and 10 merchants.  
> * **Weeks 1 to 2:** run on one corridor, help every first parcel personally, log every issue.  
> * **Weeks 3 to 4:** reduce hand-holding, measure the metrics, collect rider and merchant feedback.  
> * **End of week 4:** decision review.

## **14\. Decision After 4 Weeks**

> * **If the numbers hold:** add saved routes, then negotiation, then NIN verification tiers, merchant plans and ads, and automated messages. Later add hubs, then a second corridor and other cities.  
> * **If riders do not accept at the current price:** fix the rider price and matching before building anything else.  
> * **If merchants do not return:** investigate price, reliability and trust before adding features.

## **15\. Final Resolve**

Sendalli launches as a lean, asset-light pilot on one busy Warri/Effurun corridor, turning keke riders' existing trips into extra income by carrying small, sealed, value-capped parcels for fare-anchored fixed prices. A merchant posts a parcel with a photo and pays into escrow, an on-route rider accepts with one tap, scans the pickup QR at the roadside, makes a quick stop where the waiting receiver reads out the merchant-shared 6-digit code, and is paid instantly. We recruit the riders who already agreed through their park chairman plus 10 merchants, run it for four weeks, and judge success on hard numbers before adding anything else.
