# **Sendalli: Comprehensive Business Plan (Full Vision)**

*Hyper-local parcel delivery on existing keke routes, starting in Warri and Effurun, Nigeria. This is the full-scale version to grow into after the lean pilot succeeds.*

## **1\. Overview and Mission**

Sendalli is an app-enabled transit-logistics and micro-fulfillment network for secondary Nigerian cities. It lets small parcels ride with commercial tricycles (kekes) and minibuses that are already travelling established corridors (Refinery Road, PTI Road, Airport Road, Enerhen Junction, Jakpa). The goal is to be the affordable logistics foundation for local e-commerce, small-scale manufacturing and neighborhood retail, not a high-margin tollbooth.

## **2\. The Problem**

> * Dedicated couriers charge roughly N1,500 to N2,000 across town, which wipes out the profit on small everyday sales.  
> * Small merchants have no reliable, affordable way to deliver.  
> * Keke riders earn only from passengers (fares in Warri are about N150 to N1,000) and have daily costs such as fuel and owner remittance. They have spare capacity and stops they already make.

## **3\. The Solution**

Route-pooled delivery: a merchant or customer posts a small parcel, and an on-route keke rider carries it alongside passengers for a fare-anchored price (roughly N200 to N600). The rider earns near-pure add-on income, the customer pays far less than a courier, and Sendalli takes a small fixed fee.

## **4\. How the Service Works (Full Version)**

> 1. **Post:** The customer or business posts pickup point, drop point, a parcel photo or short video (about 10 seconds), size, and an offered price. Or one tap on a saved fixed-price route.  
> 2. **Notify:** Riders who are "On route" on that corridor are alerted with the route, media, offer and their earnings.  
> 3. **Accept, decline or counter:** Riders can accept, decline or make one counter-offer. The customer accepts one. The platform fee is locked at acceptance.  
> 4. **Escrow:** Payment goes into in-app escrow at booking.  
> 5. **Pickup:** The rider stops at the roadside and scans the pickup QR (sent to the merchant on WhatsApp or shown in the app).  
> 6. **Countdown and coordination:** The receiver is told the drop point and arrival time ("Jakpa junction in 12 minutes"). Coordination is by in-app chat, WhatsApp updates or calls.  
> 7. **Handover:** A 1 to 2 minute stop at a named drop point. The rider checks the receiver's name, takes a delivery photo, and scans the delivery QR or enters the 6-digit code.  
> 8. **Release:** Escrow releases to the rider's in-app wallet. A short dispute window (about 30 minutes) follows.

## **5\. Pricing and Fee Structure**

### **5.1 Fare-anchored price bands**

| Band | Typical keke fare | Parcel price | Rider | Sendalli |
| :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- | :---- |
| Short | N150 to N300 | N200 to N250 | N150 to N200 | N50 to N100 |
| Medium | N300 to N600 | N300 to N400 | N230 to N300 | N70 to N100 |
| Long | N600 to N1,000 | N450 to N600 | N350 to N500 | N100 |

### **5.2 Dynamic (negotiated) pricing**

> * Customers and riders can both set prices, similar to inDrive.  
> * The platform fee sits on top of the agreed price so the rider always receives exactly what was agreed.  
> * Suggested fee bands: up to N400 \= N100; N401 to N700 \= N150; N701 to N1,200 \= N200; above N1,200 \= 15% (capped at N400). Avoid sharp cliffs.  
> * Guardrails: a price floor per zone, a suggested price by distance, total shown upfront, one counter per rider, negotiation only inside the app, and the fee locked at acceptance.  
> * Businesses can save routes with fixed prices for one-tap posting, override the price for special parcels, and batch post 5 to 10 parcels at once. "Instant accept" posts can be prioritized for riders.  
> * Underpriced-post prompts: if a post is not picked up, suggest raising the price.

## **6\. Revenue Model**

> * **Per-parcel fee:** N50 to N200 depending on band (the core, volume-driven income).  
> * **Merchant plans (prepaid, recurring):** Starter about N3,000/month (promoted listing plus about 10 discounted deliveries); Business about N10,000/month (featured listing, about 40 deliveries, priority slots). Unused deliveries expire.  
> * **Fixed-price ad spots:** Featured slot about N2,000/week, sold in advance with direct-dial and WhatsApp buttons. Hyper-local product discovery listings.  
> * **Optional add-ons:** Priority slot (about N100 extra), parcel insurance (about N50 to N100).  
> * **Later:** Verification fees or bundled verification in merchant plans.

### **Illustrative fee revenue at small volumes (average fee about N120)**

| Parcels per day | Monthly fees |
| :---- | :---- |
| 10 | N36,000 |
| 25 | N90,000 |
| 50 | N180,000 |
| 100 | N360,000 |

Adding about 5 merchant plans and 2 ad slots gives roughly N30,000 more early on. At larger scale (150 to 400 parcels/day) a city can reach roughly N750,000 to N1.8M per month. These are placeholders until pilot data exists.  
**Blunt reality:** per-parcel income is thin. Verification lookups, payment gateway fees, WhatsApp costs, disputes and one lost parcel can erase it. Recurring plans and ads are the stabilizer, and a loss reserve of about N10 to N20 per parcel is recommended.

## **7\. Trust and Security Framework**

> * **Union-backed rider vetting:** riders are recruited through keke and minibus unions; the park chairman confirms each rider in-app (counts as one reference).  
> * **Tamper-evident packaging:** standard branded polybags or security tape; tampering is visible at handover and the receiver can reject.  
> * **Escrow with code/QR release:** funds stay locked until the receiver confirms with the code or QR.  
> * **Photos and timestamps:** pickup photo, delivery photo and scan times form the evidence trail for disputes.  
> * **Prohibited items list** and a value cap; no food or perishables at first.  
> * **Fast removal:** one confirmed theft means immediate suspension, with ID, plate number and chairman on file.  
> * **Loss policy:** Sendalli refunds up to the cap, quickly, funded by a reserve. A "Report a problem" button with a 30-minute window.  
> * **Lesson from food-delivery incidents (for example Glovo stories):** the damage comes from customers seeing nobody accountable. Design for the bad apple with refunds, suspensions and a paper trail.

### **7.1 Verified tiers and trust badges**

> * **Tier 1 Basic:** phone plus OTP; small parcels only (value cap around N20,000).  
> * **Tier 2 Verified:** NIN plus selfie match; higher cap.  
> * **Tier 3 Trusted:** BVN plus 3 references who are verified Sendalli users; highest caps, priority routing, "Trusted" badge.  
> * **Tier 4 Pioneer/Elite:** earned through completed jobs, low dispute rate and good ratings. Early riders and hub operators earn the "Pioneer Verified" badge.

Reference rules: references must be Tier 2 or above, must confirm in-app, are warned they are vouching, and are flagged if the person they vouched for commits fraud. Watch for vouching rings. A union leader counts as one rider reference.  
Considerations: three references add friction, so keep Tier 1 instant and tie upgrades to benefits; verify at upgrade time to save lookup costs (providers such as Smile ID, Dojah, Prembly); comply with the NDPA (consent, minimal storage, encryption; store results and reference IDs rather than raw numbers). Identity is not intent, so escrow, codes and sealed packaging always stay.

## **8\. Hubs (Later Phase)**

Hubs are partner roadside micro-shops (provision stores, chemists, phone accessory stands) used as fixed pickup and collection points.

> * **Hub earnings:** about N50 per parcel at the drop hub (released on receiver confirmation), N30 to N50 at the pickup hub, paid from the delivery price so customers pay nothing extra. Example on N450: rider N350, hub N50, Sendalli N50.  
> * **Extras:** foot traffic for the shop, a free ad listing, volume bonuses (for example N500 extra for 50 parcels a month), and a storage fee (N20 to N50/day after 24 hours, charged to the recipient).  
> * **Rules:** pay to in-app wallet after confirmation; cap parcels held; hubs are responsible while holding parcels (sealed packaging, check-in photo, small deposit or trusted-hub badge).  
> * **Why later:** hubs add recruiting, payouts and liability. They are most useful for receivers who cannot meet the keke, and they restore the fixed-point countdown experience.

## **9\. Communication Layer**

> * WhatsApp is where users already are. Use Sendalli's own WhatsApp Business number for live updates ("Rider picked up your parcel, arriving in 12 minutes").  
> * Two QR codes: a pickup QR to the merchant and a delivery QR to the receiver. Each is single-use, tied to one parcel and expiring. Keep a 6-digit backup code for poor networks or cracked screens.  
> * Keep chat and negotiation in the app, hide phone numbers until booking is confirmed, and consider number masking or in-app calling.  
> * Costs: WhatsApp Business API charges per conversation and needs approved templates. Send only key messages.

## **10\. Technical Architecture**

> * **Single-app MVP:** role-based dashboards (customer/merchant and rider) to save capital and iterate fast.  
> * **Modular backend:** role-separated APIs so it can later split into Sendalli Customer/Discovery and Sendalli Partner apps.  
> * **Low-bandwidth design:** small screens, compressed photos, 10-second video cap, saved progress, SMS autofill OTP with WhatsApp fallback.  
> * **Key modules:** escrow wallet and payouts, posting and matching (route-based alerts), QR/OTP handover, photo and timestamp evidence, verification tiers, dispute console, ad/plan management.

## **11\. Onboarding (Frictionless)**

Principle: reach the first action in under 60 seconds and ask for more only when a limit is hit.

> * **Customers (3 screens):** phone plus OTP, first name, land on "Post a parcel" with a 3-tap walkthrough.  
> * **Merchants (4 screens):** phone plus OTP, business name and shop photo, pick a saved route and price, send a test parcel with a first-delivery credit.  
> * **Riders (5 screens, agent-assisted):** phone plus OTP, name/park/vehicle, selfie and plate photo, union leader one-tap approval, go live with a low cap. NIN plus selfie unlocks higher-value parcels and the Pioneer badge.  
> * **Hubs (3 screens):** phone plus OTP, shop name/photo/location pin, accept the handover guide.  
> * **Friction killers:** OTP auto-read, saved progress, progress bar, English plus Pidgin, agent mode, skip buttons, contextual upgrade prompts.  
> * **Sequence:** onboard supply first (riders, then merchants), do the first 20 to 30 onboardings personally, and open to customers only when enough riders are active on the corridor.

## **12\. Growth Strategy and Moat**

> * **Asset-light replication:** new cities launch with near-zero capital by partnering with keke unions and, later, roadside shops.  
> * **Speed and supply lock-in:** saturate a city quickly through union partnerships. Be honest: this moat is weak, since union deals are non-exclusive and larger players (Gig Logistics, Kwik) could copy. The stronger moats are rider density, merchant habit and a trusted track record.  
> * **Demand growth:** short-form video (TikTok/Instagram) showing real local savings, merchant WhatsApp groups, market associations, park word-of-mouth.  
> * **Rider retention:** fast payouts (same-day withdrawal), bonuses (for example first 10 parcels, or 5 parcels a week), priority for high acceptance.

## **13\. Key Risks and Mitigations**

| Risk | Mitigation |
| :---- | :---- |
| Thin per-parcel margin | Merchant plans, ads, tiered fees, loss reserve, lean costs |
| Rider supply and habit (open app, go On route) | Simple one-tap flow, fast payouts, bonuses, park word-of-mouth |
| Matching (no keke passing when a parcel is posted) | Dense supply on one busy corridor, route-based alerts only |
| Off-platform leakage | Escrow, repeat convenience, hide numbers, saved routes, plans |
| Theft, loss, liability | Value cap, sealed bags, photos, escrow release on code, loss reserve, suspensions |
| Cold start on every route and city | Corridor-by-corridor launch; supply before demand |
| Copycats | Density, habit and trust rather than speed alone |
| Regulation and union politics | Chairman buy-in, prohibited items list, compliance with NDPA |
| Feature overload | Phase the build; prove the core first |

## **14\. Phased Roadmap**

> 1. **Phase 1 (0 to 2 months): Lean pilot** on one corridor (see the Lean Start document).  
> 2. **Phase 2:** saved routes, batch posting, merchant plans, basic ad spots.  
> 3. **Phase 3:** negotiation and counters, NIN verification tier, badges, automated WhatsApp updates.  
> 4. **Phase 4:** hubs, BVN plus references (Trusted tier), insurance, express slots.  
> 5. **Phase 5:** more corridors in Warri/Effurun, then other secondary cities, with a possible split into two native apps.

## **15\. Success Metrics**

> * Completed parcels per day and per rider per day (target 2 or more)  
> * Time from post to acceptance (under 5 minutes) and accept rate (over 70%)  
> * Repeat merchants (at least half sending weekly)  
> * Problem/dispute/off-app rate (under 3%)  
> * Average fee per parcel and cost per parcel (payment fees, verification, messaging, losses)  
> * Rider retention and payout speed

## **16\. Honest Assessment**

Strengths: real pain point, asset-light, underserved market, riders with a clear motive and early verbal buy-in. Weaknesses: thin margins, dependence on volume, fragile supply, weak moat and liability exposure. The full feature set above is a destination, not a starting point. Prove that on-route riders reliably carry parcels at a price merchants will pay, then build outward.
